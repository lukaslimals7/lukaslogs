leave no trace

On disappointment, the burden of existing for others, and Fyodor Dostoevsky's line about walking on your broken foot.

This is one of the lines I come back to when the person I love most disappoints me.

People disappoint so much that it starts to feel like I am the reason for this wrenching feeling in my chest. Not them. Me.

We live in a world that is materialistic at its core. One small awkwardness — a misplaced word, a silence held too long — and people say things they know will kill you from inside. No matter what you do. No matter how much effort you put in. It is never enough. They make it clear that even asking for help, or even existing, is a burden to them. They say it to your face.

There is a philosophy here that I keep returning to. If love is measured by what it costs the other person, then most of us are bankrupt before we begin. If affection is conditional on convenience, then it is not affection at all. It is transaction. And transactions leave scars.

That is the tragedy most of us carry — not that people leave, but that they let you know your presence was never wanted.

So this quote comes in handy:

"Walk on your broken foot and leave no trace of your hand on anyone's shoulder."

— Fyodor Dostoevsky

Walk on your broken foot. Carry your own pain. Do not lean on someone who has already told you, with their face, that you are heavy.
