nothing to anyone

On Kafka, expectation as burden, and the freedom of being nothing to anyone.

This Kafka line keeps coming back to me:

"I am nothing to anyone and that's my greatest freedom."

— Franz Kafka

It's heavy. I wonder what Franz must have felt to write it. Lonely and liberating at the same time — and now he means a lot to so many people, simply because he wrote.

Being nothing to anyone is like walking into an empty house where no one knows your name. You can rearrange the furniture however you want. No one comes in to tell you the sofa belongs against the other wall.

Expectation is a burden. Freedom isn't what makes sense; it's choosing what you want to do. The urge to be understood is exhausting. But in the end, if you have yourself, you don't need anyone to feel full or understood. I learned that the hard way.

I hope whoever reads this gets it sooner, without suffering too much.
